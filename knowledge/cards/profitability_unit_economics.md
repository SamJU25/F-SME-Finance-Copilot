# Profitability & Unit Economics

## Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
<!-- tools: tool_calculate_ltv_cac, tool_calculate_cac_payback -->
**What it is:** The average sales and marketing spend needed to win one new customer. CAC = sales and marketing cost ÷ new customers acquired.
**Why it matters:** Growth that costs more to acquire than customers are worth destroys cash.
**Healthy vs warning:** Healthy: CAC stays stable or falls as the business scales. Warning: CAC rising each quarter while customer value stays flat.
**What to do:** Track CAC by channel and shift spend toward channels with the lowest cost per retained customer.
**In the app:** An input to LTV/CAC and CAC payback.

## LTV to CAC Ratio
<!-- tools: tool_calculate_ltv_cac | policy: unit_economics_policy -->
**What it is:** Customer lifetime value (gross profit a customer brings over the relationship) divided by the cost to acquire them.
**Why it matters:** It shows whether marketing spend creates value or burns cash.
**Healthy vs warning:** 4.0x or more supports expansion, 3.0x to 4.0x is PRIME, 1.5x to 3.0x is VIABLE_MARGINAL, and below 1.5x is UNSUSTAINABLE_CAC.
**What to do:** Improve the ratio by raising retention and margin, or lowering acquisition cost, before scaling spend.
**In the app:** Ask with customer value inputs and acquisition cost.

## CAC Payback Period
<!-- tools: tool_calculate_cac_payback | policy: unit_economics_policy -->
**What it is:** The number of months of gross profit from a customer needed to recover what it cost to acquire them.
**Why it matters:** Until payback, acquisition spend is cash tied up; long paybacks strain working capital.
**Healthy vs warning:** Up to 12 months is RAPID, up to 18 months COMMERCIAL_STANDARD, up to 24 months ELEVATED, and above 24 months a PROLONGED_CASH_TRAP.
**What to do:** Shorten payback with upfront or annual billing, higher-margin plans or cheaper channels.
**In the app:** Ask with CAC and monthly gross profit per customer.

## Net Revenue Retention (NRR)
<!-- tools: tool_calculate_nrr | policy: unit_economics_policy -->
**What it is:** Revenue this period from last period's customers, including upgrades and minus cancellations and downgrades, as a percentage of their starting revenue.
**Why it matters:** High retention means growth compounds without new acquisition spend.
**Healthy vs warning:** 120% or more is BEST_IN_CLASS, 105% or more STRONG, 95% or more STABLE, and below 95% NET_REVENUE_ATTRITION.
**What to do:** Investigate why customers leave and reduce churn before increasing acquisition spend.
**In the app:** Ask with starting revenue, expansion, contraction and churn.

## Contribution Margin
<!-- tools: tool_calculate_break_even, tool_calculate_break_even_units -->
**What it is:** Revenue minus variable costs, either per unit or as a percentage of sales. It is what each sale contributes toward fixed costs and profit.
**Why it matters:** It decides how many sales are needed to break even and how pricing changes affect profit.
**Healthy vs warning:** A higher contribution margin means fewer sales are needed to cover fixed costs. A thin margin makes the business sensitive to discounts and cost increases.
**What to do:** Review discounts and variable costs before raising sales targets.
**In the app:** Used in break-even revenue and break-even units.

## Return on Equity and the DuPont Breakdown
<!-- tools: tool_calculate_dupont_roe | policy: unit_economics_policy -->
**What it is:** Return on equity (ROE) is net profit divided by owners' equity. The DuPont method splits it into profit margin × asset turnover × leverage.
**Why it matters:** The breakdown shows whether returns come from profitability, efficient use of assets, or borrowing.
**Healthy vs warning:** ROE of 20% or more is EXCEPTIONAL, 12% or more STRONG, 5% or more MODERATE, and below 5% SUBPAR. High ROE driven mainly by leverage is riskier than high ROE from margins.
**What to do:** Use the weakest of the three components to decide where to improve.
**In the app:** Ask with net income, revenue, assets and equity.

## Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) and Return on Assets (ROA)
<!-- tools: tool_calculate_roic, tool_calculate_roa | policy: unit_economics_policy -->
**What it is:** ROIC is after-tax operating profit divided by capital invested by lenders and owners. ROA is net profit divided by total assets.
**Why it matters:** They show how efficiently the business turns money and assets into profit.
**Healthy vs warning:** ROIC of 15% or more is EXCEPTIONAL, 10% or more STRONG, 6% or more MODERATE, and below 6% VALUE_DESTRUCTIVE. ROA of 10% or more is EXCELLENT and 5% or more HEALTHY.
**What to do:** Compare ROIC with your cost of capital; growth only creates value when ROIC is higher.
**In the app:** Ask with operating profit, tax rate and invested capital, or net income and assets.

## Economic Value Added (EVA)
<!-- tools: tool_calculate_eva, tool_calculate_wacc | policy: unit_economics_policy -->
**What it is:** Operating profit after tax minus a charge for all capital used, calculated with the weighted average cost of capital (WACC).
**Why it matters:** A business can report accounting profit yet still fail to earn enough to justify the money invested in it.
**Healthy vs warning:** Positive EVA means real economic profit. Zero means returns exactly cover the cost of capital. Negative means the capital would earn more elsewhere.
**What to do:** Raise returns on existing assets or release capital tied up in low-return activities.
**In the app:** Ask with operating profit, invested capital and cost of capital.
